Facebook can feel crowded and complicated, but for many small businesses it is still one of the most useful places to be. It is where a lot of people look up local businesses, check opening hours, read recommendations, join community groups and ask for advice.

This guide covers the basics: getting your Page right, deciding what to post, using Groups, and running your first simple ads without wasting money.

## Step 1: Set up a complete Page

Your Facebook Page is often the first thing people see when they search for you. Treat it like a mini website. Check that you have:

- **A clear profile picture**, usually your logo, that is easy to read when small.
- **A cover image** that shows what you do, not just a pretty photo.
- **The right category**, so Facebook understands what kind of business you are.
- **A short, clear description** of who you help and how.
- **Contact details, location and opening hours**, kept up to date.
- **An action button**, such as Book now, Send message or Call now.
- **Your services or products listed**, with simple descriptions.

Ask a friend to look at your Page for ten seconds and tell you what you do. If they cannot, simplify it.

## Step 2: Get your accounts organized

Use **Meta Business Suite** to manage your Facebook Page and Instagram account in one place. You can schedule posts, reply to messages and see basic results without switching apps.

One important tip: make sure **your business owns its Page and ad account**. If you work with a freelancer or agency, give them access as a partner instead of letting them create accounts under their own name. That way, you never lose your Page, your audience or your ad history if you change suppliers.

## Step 3: Post with a purpose

You do not need to post every day. You do need to post with a reason. A healthy mix for most small businesses looks like this:

- **Helpful tips** that answer common customer questions.
- **Behind the scenes** of your work, your team and your space.
- **Local and community content**, such as events you support or partners you work with.
- **Offers and news**, like new products, seasonal menus or booking dates.
- **Real customer stories**, shared only with their permission.

For example, a hair salon might post a hair care tip on Monday, a quick video of a color transformation on Wednesday and open slots for the weekend on Friday.

Keep posts short, use real photos where you can, and always tell people what to do next: call, book, message or visit.

## Step 4: Use Groups the right way

Facebook Groups can be great for local businesses, because neighbors often ask for recommendations there.

- **Join groups where your customers spend time**, such as local community, parent or hobby groups.
- **Read the rules first.** Many groups do not allow direct promotion.
- **Be genuinely helpful.** Answer questions and share advice without always pushing a sale.
- **Think about your own group** if your customers have something to talk about, like a running club for a sports shop or a recipe group for a food brand.

People remember the business that helped them, not the one that spammed the feed.

## Step 5: Run your first simple ads

Ads let you reach people beyond your followers. Start simple.

1. **Pick one goal.** For example, more messages, more website visits or more bookings. Choose the matching objective in Ads Manager.
2. **Choose a clear audience.** For local businesses, start with a location around your shop or service area, then add age or interests only if they truly matter.
3. **Make one clear offer.** "Book your first class" or "Order your free sample" works better than a general "We are great".
4. **Create two or three versions.** Try different images, videos or headlines and see which one people respond to.
5. **Set a daily budget you are comfortable with** while you learn. Start small, then increase spending on what works.
6. **Give it time.** Let ads run long enough to see a pattern before you change everything.

The Boost button is fine for quick tests, but Ads Manager gives you more control over who sees your ads and what you pay for.

## Step 6: Track results before you spend

Before running ads to your website, set up the **Meta Pixel** or the Conversions API so you can see which ads lead to bookings or sales. Add tracking tags (UTM parameters) to your links so your website analytics can show where visitors came from.

Decide in advance what counts as a result. A like is nice. A booking, an inquiry or a sale is what pays the bills.

## Step 7: Reply quickly and kindly

Messages and comments are where many sales happen. Check your inbox regularly, set up **saved replies** for common questions like prices or opening hours, and answer reviews politely, including the negative ones. A calm, helpful reply often says more about your business than the review itself.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **An unfinished Page** with old hours, missing contact details or no description.
- **Only posting offers**, which trains people to scroll past.
- **Spamming groups** with links and sales posts.
- **Boosting random posts** without a goal or a clear offer.
- **Changing ads every day** before they have had a chance to show results.
- **Letting someone else own your accounts.**

## A simple monthly routine

- Update your Page details and pinned post.
- Plan the month's posts using the content mix above.
- Spend a little time in two or three relevant groups each week.
- Review your ads: keep what works, pause what does not.
- Check which posts and ads led to messages, bookings or sales.
