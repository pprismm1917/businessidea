Posting on Instagram without a plan feels busy, but it rarely builds anything. You spend hours on content, the likes come and go, and you are still not sure if any of it brings in customers.

A content strategy fixes that. It is not a 40-page document. It is a short set of decisions about **why** you post, **who** it is for, **what** you post and **how often**. Here is the process we use with brands, step by step.

## Step 1: Decide what Instagram is for

Pick one main goal for the next three months. Not five. One.

- **Awareness:** more of the right people discover you.
- **Inquiries:** people message you or book a call.
- **Sales:** people buy a product or visit your shop.
- **Community:** existing customers stay engaged and come back.

A local bakery might want pre-orders for weekend cakes. A business coach might want discovery calls. Those two accounts should look very different, because they are trying to make different things happen.

Write your goal at the top of your plan. Every post should help it, directly or indirectly.

## Step 2: Get specific about your audience

"Everyone" is not an audience. Write one short paragraph about the person you most want to reach. Answer these questions:

- What do they want right now?
- What frustrates or worries them?
- What questions do they ask before they buy?
- What would make them stop scrolling?

Your best source is real conversations. Look at your DMs, emails, reviews and the questions people ask in person. Those questions are free content ideas, written in your customers' own words.

## Step 3: Choose three or four content pillars

Content pillars are the themes you come back to again and again. They keep your feed focused and make planning much faster. A simple set that works for most businesses:

1. **Teach:** tips, how-tos and answers to common questions.
2. **Show:** behind the scenes, your process, your team, how things are made.
3. **Build trust:** real reviews (shared with permission), before-and-afters, the thinking behind your work.
4. **Personality:** your story, your values, the people behind the brand.

For example, a fitness studio could post a form tip (Teach), a look at the 6 a.m. class setup (Show), a member's story told with their permission (Build trust) and why the founder opened the studio (Personality).

## Step 4: Match each pillar to the right format

Different formats do different jobs. In our experience:

- **Reels** are your best tool for reaching new people. Use them for quick tips, processes and anything with movement.
- **Carousels** are great for teaching. People save them to read later, and saved posts keep working for you.
- **Stories** are for daily connection. Use polls, questions and quick updates to stay close to the followers you already have.
- **Single image posts** work well for announcements, quotes and launches.

You do not need every format every week. Start with the two that fit your goal and your team best.

## Step 5: Set a rhythm you can actually keep

Consistency beats volume. Three good posts a week that you can keep up for months will do more than daily posting that stops after two weeks.

A simple starting rhythm:

- Monday: a carousel that teaches something useful
- Wednesday: a Reel showing your process or a quick tip
- Friday: a Reel or post that builds trust or shows personality
- Most days: a few Stories

Make it easier by **batching**. Plan all posts for the month in one session, film several videos in one afternoon, and write captions in one sitting. Then schedule everything so you are not scrambling every morning.

## Step 6: Make your profile do the selling

Great content brings people to your profile. Your profile decides whether they take the next step. Check these:

- **Name field:** include what you do, not only your brand name.
- **Bio:** who you help, what you do and what to do next. For example: "Custom wedding cakes in Leeds. Order 4 weeks ahead. Tap below to check dates."
- **Link:** send people to one clear page, such as a booking page or a simple link page with your top two or three options.
- **Highlights:** Services, FAQ, How it works and Reviews. Keep the covers clean and on-brand.
- **Pinned posts:** pin the three posts that best explain what you do and why people should trust you.

## Step 7: Review monthly, not daily

Checking numbers every hour leads to panic decisions. Once a month, look at the bigger picture:

- Which posts were **saved** or **shared** the most?
- Which posts led to **profile visits**, **link taps** or **DMs**?
- Which topics started real conversations with potential customers?

Do more of what led to conversations and inquiries, and less of what only collected likes. Then adjust next month's plan.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Posting only about offers.** If every post is "buy now", people stop paying attention. Give value first.
- **Copying trends that do not fit.** A trend is only useful if it connects to your audience and your message.
- **Changing your look every week.** Use the same fonts, colors and style so people recognize you instantly. Our guide on [brand consistency](/blog/brand-consistency/) explains why this matters.
- **Ignoring comments and DMs.** Instagram is social. Reply, ask questions and start conversations.
- **Judging a plan too early.** Give a new approach at least a month before you decide it does not work.

## Your quick checklist

- One clear goal for the next three months
- A short description of your ideal follower
- Three or four content pillars
- Two main formats that suit your goal
- A weekly rhythm you can keep
- A profile with a clear bio, link and highlights
- A monthly review in your calendar
