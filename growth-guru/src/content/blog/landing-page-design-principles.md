A landing page has one job: to get a visitor to take one specific action. That might be booking a call, joining a waitlist, downloading a guide or buying a product.

Many landing pages fail not because the offer is bad, but because the page is confusing, slow or trying to do too many things at once. Here are ten principles we use when designing pages that turn visitors into leads and customers.

## 1. One page, one goal

Decide the single action you want people to take, then design everything around it.

- Remove or reduce the main menu, so visitors are not pulled away.
- Use **one main call to action**, repeated in a few places.
- Cut sections that do not help someone make that decision.

If you have two very different offers, build two pages.

## 2. Match the message to the click

People arrive from somewhere: an ad, an email, a social post. The page should continue that exact conversation. If your ad says "Free 30-minute website review", the headline should say the same thing, not "Welcome to our agency".

When the message changes between the click and the page, people often feel lost and leave.

## 3. Write a headline people understand in seconds

Your headline should tell visitors what they get and why it matters. A simple formula: **the result + who it is for + how**.

- Weak: "Innovative solutions for modern businesses"
- Better: "Websites that help local clinics get more bookings"

Add a short subheading that explains how it works or what makes you different. Clear always beats clever.

## 4. Put the essentials above the fold

The first screen should answer three questions: what is this, is it for me, and what do I do next? Include:

- Your headline and subheading
- A clear call-to-action button
- A visual that shows the product, the service or the result
- A simple trust cue, such as a real review or a key benefit

## 5. Make your call to action obvious and specific

Buttons should be easy to spot and easy to understand.

- **Use specific words.** "Book my free call" is clearer than "Submit" or "Learn more".
- **Make it stand out.** Use a strong contrasting color that is not used for much else on the page.
- **Repeat it.** Add the button again after key sections, so people never have to scroll back up.
- **Make it easy to tap.** On phones, buttons should be large enough to press with a thumb.

## 6. Show, do not just tell

Instead of describing how great your service is, show it.

- Real photos of your work, team or product
- Short videos that explain the offer
- A simple "How it works" section with three clear steps
- Screenshots or examples of what customers receive

Visuals help people picture themselves using what you offer.

## 7. Answer objections before they are asked

Think about what makes people hesitate, then answer it on the page. Common questions include:

- How much does it cost?
- How long does it take?
- What happens after I sign up?
- Can I cancel or change my plan?

A short FAQ section near the bottom can remove those last doubts.

## 8. Use honest proof

Trust is everything on a landing page, and it is easy to break.

- Use **real reviews and testimonials**, shared with permission.
- Show **examples of real work** or clearly label samples and concepts.
- Mention genuine credentials, partners or awards only if you have them.

Never invent numbers, logos or quotes. Visitors notice, and fake proof does more damage than no proof.

## 9. Keep forms short

Every extra field gives people another reason to stop.

- **Ask only for what you need** right now, often just name and email.
- **Use a multi-step form** if you need more details, starting with the easiest questions.
- **Explain what happens next**, such as "We will reply by email to arrange a time."
- **Show a clear success message** after submitting, so people know it worked.

## 10. Make it fast and mobile-first

Many visitors will see your page on a phone, sometimes on a slow connection. A slow page loses people before they read a word.

- Compress images and use modern formats.
- Avoid heavy scripts, sliders and pop-ups you do not need.
- Test the page on a real phone, not only on a desktop screen.
- Keep text readable without zooming.

## Measure, then improve one thing at a time

Set up conversion tracking before you launch, so you know how many visitors take action. Then test one change at a time: a new headline, a different button text or a new hero image. Give each test enough time to show a clear pattern before you decide.

Small, steady improvements usually beat a full redesign every few months.

## Landing page checklist

- One clear goal and one main call to action
- A headline that matches the ad or link people clicked
- A first screen that explains what, who and what next
- Specific, easy-to-tap buttons
- Real visuals and honest proof
- A short FAQ that answers common doubts
- A short form with a clear success message
- Fast loading and a great experience on phones
- Conversion tracking set up from day one
