On TikTok, people decide very quickly whether to keep watching or swipe away. That first moment is your **hook**: what you say, what you show and what you write on screen before anything else happens.

You can have a helpful, well-made video, but if the opening does not grab attention, most people will never see the good part. The good news is that hooks are a skill. You can learn them, practice them and test them.

## What makes a hook work

In our experience, strong hooks share three things:

- **Clarity:** the viewer instantly knows what the video is about.
- **Relevance:** it speaks to someone specific, so the right people think "this is for me".
- **Curiosity:** it opens a question that the rest of the video answers.

Think of a hook as a small promise. "Here is something you want to know, and I am about to show you." The rest of the video has to keep that promise.

## 10 hook formats you can use this week

Here are hook styles that work for many kinds of businesses, with examples you can adapt.

1. **The bold statement.** Say something direct that challenges a common belief. "Your website is not losing customers because of the design."
2. **The mistake call-out.** People hate making mistakes. "Stop doing this if you run a small café."
3. **The specific question.** Ask the question your customer is already thinking. "Why does your skin feel dry even after moisturizer?"
4. **The finished result first.** Show the end result in the first frame, then explain how you got there. A cleaner can open on the spotless kitchen, then rewind to the before.
5. **The how-to promise.** Be clear about the value. "How to plan a full week of content in one afternoon."
6. **The short list.** Lists feel easy to watch. "Three things I would never put on a restaurant menu."
7. **The point of view.** Put the viewer in a situation they recognize. "POV: you finally found a hairdresser who listens."
8. **The behind the scenes.** Curiosity about how things are made is powerful. "Here is what happens after you order one of our candles."
9. **The myth buster.** Correct something your audience gets wrong. "You do not need to post every day to grow."
10. **The direct call-out.** Name exactly who the video is for. "If you sell handmade jewelry, watch this before your next launch."

Write five or six hooks for every video idea, then pick the strongest. The first idea is rarely the best one.

## Make the first frame do the work

A hook is not only words. The opening image matters just as much.

- **Start in the middle of the action.** Skip "Hi guys, welcome back". Open with the pour, the cut, the reveal or the reaction.
- **Add movement.** A hand entering the frame, a quick zoom or a product being placed down catches the eye better than a still shot.
- **Put the hook on screen as text.** Many people scroll with the sound off. Short, bold text at the top of the video helps them understand right away.
- **Keep text in the safe area.** Avoid the bottom and right edges, where captions and buttons cover the screen.
- **Leave your logo intro out.** Brand the video with your style, colors and voice instead.

## Say it and show it

The strongest openings line up three layers at once: the words you say, the text on screen and what the viewer sees. For example, a bakery might say "This is why our croissants take three days" while the screen shows layered dough being folded and the text reads "3 days for 1 croissant?". Each layer supports the others, so the message lands even if someone only catches one of them.

## Keep the promise

A clever hook that leads nowhere does more harm than good. People feel tricked, and in our experience they are less likely to watch your next video.

- **Deliver the payoff quickly.** If you promise three tips, get to tip one fast.
- **Cut every slow moment.** Trim pauses, repeated sentences and long introductions.
- **End with a next step.** Ask a question, invite a comment or point people to your profile.

## How to test your hooks

You do not need to guess what works. Run small, simple tests.

1. **Film one video with two or three different openings.** Keep the rest of the video the same.
2. **Post the versions on different days**, at similar times.
3. **Check your analytics.** Look at how long people watched and where they dropped off. If many viewers leave in the first seconds, the hook is the problem.
4. **Keep a hook swipe file.** Save hooks that worked for you and openings you liked from other creators, then reuse the structure with your own topic.

Treat each result as a clue, not a final verdict. One video can underperform for many reasons, so look for patterns across several posts.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Slow starts.** Greetings, long intros and "so, today I want to talk about" lose people.
- **Vague hooks.** "Some tips for your business" does not tell anyone why they should care.
- **Clickbait.** Promising something the video does not deliver hurts trust.
- **Tiny or messy text.** If it is hard to read on a phone, it does not help.
- **Copying a hook without the context.** A trending format only works if it fits your message and your audience.
