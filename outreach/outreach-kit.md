## 1. Cold email sequence (3 emails, 10 days)

### 1.1 Rules before you send (CAN-SPAM, the US email law)

- **Send from your real name and address:** "Moe, Picket Digital" `<moe@picketdigital.com>`. Replies go to the same address.
- **Honest subject line.** It must match what is inside. Never write "re:" or "fwd:" on a first email.
- **It is clearly an offer.** Our emails say what we sell. Never pretend to be a customer, a friend or a neighbor.
- **Real mailing address in every email.** Replace `[mailing address]` with a real postal address where you receive mail: your real business address (in your country is fine) or a mailbox you rent. If you rent a US mailbox, call it a "mailing address". **Never call it an office.**
- **Easy opt-out in every email:** "Reply 'no' and I won't email again."
- **When someone says no, stop. Same day.** The law gives you 10 business days. Do it the same day. Mark them `do_not_contact` in `prospects.csv`. Never email them again, from any address.
- **Plain text only.** No images, no attachments, no open-tracking. **One link maximum:** picketdigital.com.
- Use only email addresses the business **publishes itself** (on its website, Facebook page, or a listing it controls). Never buy email lists.

*This is a simple summary, not legal advice.*

## 3. Phone call script and objections

### 3.1 Call rules

- Call only the **business phone number** the company publishes (website, Facebook, Google, BBB).
- Call **Monday–Friday, 9:00 am–6:00 pm Texas time**. Texas law allows sales calls only from 9 am to 9 pm (Sunday: noon to 9 pm). Stay well inside that. **No Sunday calls.**
- Owners are often on a job site in the morning. Good times to try: **12:00–1:00 pm** and **4:00–6:00 pm**.
- **Dial by hand.** No robocalls, no auto-dialers, no recorded or AI voice messages.
- Maximum **2 call attempts** and **1 voicemail** per company.
- If they say "don't call me", say sorry, hang up, and mark `do_not_contact` **right away**.
- Speak slowly. Smile while you talk (people can hear it). An accent is fine. Clear and honest matters more.

### 3.2 The 30-second opener

> **You:** Hi, is this {{first_name}}?
> *(wait)*
> **You:** Hi {{first_name}}, this is Moe from Picket Digital. I know you're busy, so I'll be quick. Thirty seconds, OK?
> *(wait)*
> **You:** We build websites only for fence and deck companies in Dallas–Fort Worth. I saw that {{company}} [doesn't have a website / only has a Facebook page / has a website that's hard to use on a phone].
> I'd like to build you a **free draft website**: your photos, your cities, and a quote form that texts your phone. You don't pay anything to see it. If you like it, we talk. If not, no problem.
> Can I send it to you?

**If yes:**
> **You:** Great! What's the best email for you?
> *(write it down)*
> **You:** Is it OK if I text you when the draft is ready? What's the best number?
> *(write down the number AND that they said yes to texts, with the date. See section 4.)*
> **You:** Perfect. I'll have it ready by [day] at [time] Texas time. Thanks, {{first_name}}!

### 3.3 Common objections

**"I get enough work from referrals."**
> That's great. It means you do good work. A lot of people who get your name from a friend still look you up online before they call. A simple site with your photos and reviews helps them say yes. The draft is free. Want to see it anyway?

**"How much?"**
> It's $199 a month plus $299 setup. Right now my first 3 DFW partners pay $0 setup. That includes hosting, your domain in your name, and edits whenever you need them. There's a 12-month minimum, and you see everything in writing before you sign. And you only pay after you approve the design. If you prefer to pay once, I can quote that too. Want me to build the free draft first?

*(When the 3 launch spots are gone, drop the "$0 setup" sentence.)*

**"I already have a guy." / "My nephew does it."**
> Good. If it works for you, keep him. Just two questions: when a homeowner asks for a quote on your site, does it text your phone right away? And when you want new photos up, how fast does it happen? If you want, I'll build the free draft anyway and you can compare. No pressure.

**"Where are you located?" / "Are you in Texas?"**
> No, I'm not in Texas. We're a small remote team and we work Texas business hours, so you can reach us by phone, text or email during your workday. Everything is done online, and your domain is registered in your name.

If they ask which country, **tell them the truth**. If you work alone right now, say "I" and not "we". Never make the team sound bigger than it is.

**Short answers for other common lines:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Not interested." | "No problem. Can I ask: is it because you're busy enough, or because you already have a website you like?" *(one question only, then thank them and end the call)* |
| "Send me an email." | "Sure. What's the best email? I'll send one short email. You can just reply yes or no." |
| "Is this a scam?" | "Fair question. You pay nothing to see the draft. If you like it, you get all the terms in writing before you sign. If you don't, you just say no." |
| "Don't call me again." | "Understood, I'll remove your number. Sorry to bother you." *(mark `do_not_contact` now)* |

## 4. Text and WhatsApp follow-up (only after they say OK)

### 4.1 Why we never send cold texts

**Never text a company that has not agreed to it.** Only text when:
- the owner said "yes, text me" **on a call**, or
- the owner **replied to your email** and gave you a number or asked you to text.

Why this rule is strict:
- **US federal law (TCPA):** marketing texts to a cell phone without permission can lead to lawsuits of **$500–$1,500 per text**. Many small fence owners use their personal cell as the business number.
- **Texas law (Senate Bill 140, in force since September 1, 2025):** marketing texts are now covered by the Texas telephone solicitation law. Texting people you have no relationship with can require registering with the Texas Secretary of State (a fee plus a $10,000 bond). Penalties can be up to $5,000 per violation, and people can sue.
- Phone carriers block unknown business texts anyway.

*This is a simple summary, not legal advice.*

**Write the permission down.** In `prospects.csv` (notes column), write for example: `texts OK: said yes on call 2026-10-14`.

**WhatsApp:** most US owners use normal text messages (SMS). Use WhatsApp only if the owner asks for it.

Text rules: Monday–Friday, 9 am–6 pm Texas time. Maximum **1 reminder** if they don't answer. If they reply "STOP" (or anything like it), stop forever.

### 4.2 Text templates

**T1. Right after a call where they said yes**
```
Hi {{first_name}}, it's Moe from Picket Digital, thanks for the chat! Your free draft will be ready by {{day_time}} Texas time. If you can, send 3–5 photos of your work here. (Reply STOP anytime and I won't text again.)
```

**T2. After they said yes by email and gave you their number**
```
Hi {{first_name}}, Moe from Picket Digital here (you replied to my email about the free website draft). Thanks! I'll text you the link by {{day_time}} Texas time. (Reply STOP anytime and I won't text again.)
```

**T3. Draft is ready**
```
Hi {{first_name}}, your free website draft is ready: {{draft_link}}
Take a look on your phone. Tell me what to change. Nothing to pay unless you approve it.
```

**T4. One reminder (2–3 days after T3, only if no answer)**
```
Hi {{first_name}}, did you get a chance to look at the draft? Happy to change anything. If now's not a good time, just tell me and I'll stop.
```

## 5. When someone says "yes"

### 5.1 Answer fast

Reply **within 2 hours** (Texas time), even if it's just "Great, thanks!" A fast answer shows them how you work.

### 5.2 Ask for the information (email template)

```
Great, thanks {{first_name}}! To build your draft, please send me:

1. Company name (exactly how you want it shown)
2. Phone number for the website
3. Email where quote requests should go
4. Cities you serve (your top 5–10)
5. Your services (for example: cedar privacy, iron, chain link, gates, decks, repairs, staining)
6. 5–10 photos of your work (phone photos are fine)
7. Your logo, if you have one (no logo is OK)
8. Your Facebook or Google page link, if you have one

Only 1 to 4 are needed to start. Send what you have, and I'll have your draft ready by {{day_time}} Texas time.

Moe
```

Nice to have (use only what **they tell you**): years in business, free estimates, financing, warranty, licensed and insured. Never write "licensed and insured" unless the owner says it is true.

**No photos?** Ask: "Can I use the photos from your Facebook or Google page for the draft?" Wait for a yes. If there are no photos at all, use clearly marked placeholder images ("sample photo") and replace them later. **Never show someone else's fence as their work.**

### 5.3 The 48-hour promise

- The 48 hours start when you have items 1–4 and some photos (or permission to use their Facebook/Google photos).
- Always give an exact deadline: "**Thursday by 5 pm Texas time**", not "soon".
- If you will be late, tell them **before** the deadline, not after.
- **Keep the draft private.** Use a private preview link. Don't link it from picketdigital.com. Set it to `noindex` so Google doesn't show it. A public site with their name that they didn't approve looks like you are pretending to be them.

### 5.4 Send the draft (email template)

```
Hi {{first_name}},

Your free draft is ready: {{draft_link}}

Please open it on your phone, because that's how most of your customers will see it.

Three quick questions:
1. Is anything wrong or missing?
2. Which photos should go first?
3. Should I add any cities?

Nothing to pay unless you approve it.

Moe
```

### 5.5 After the draft

- **They want changes:** make them fast (same day if you can). Repeat until they are happy.
- **They approve:** send the terms **in writing** before any payment: price, 12-month minimum, what's included, domain registered in their name. If they get the $0 setup launch offer, explain the deal clearly: it is **in exchange for honest feedback and permission to show their site as an example of our work** (this is what picketdigital.com says).
- **Status `won`** only after they sign.
- **They say no:** thank them, mark `lost`, and **delete the draft within a week.** Don't leave a site with their name online.

## 6. Daily routine, volume limits and tracking

### 6.1 Volume limits for a new domain

picketdigital.com is a new domain. Your emails land in the inbox now. **Keep it that way: go slow.** Spam filters watch new domains closely.

| Weeks | New first emails per day | All emails per day (with follow-ups and replies) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **10–15** | up to 25 |
| 3–4 | 15–20 | up to 35 |
| 5 and later | 20–30 | up to 50 |

**Go to the next level only if all of these are true:**
- Fewer than 3 in 100 emails bounce (come back "undeliverable").
- Nobody complained or said "spam".
- Your weekly test email to a Gmail and an Outlook address still lands in the inbox.

**Stop signs (pause 2–3 days and send only replies):**
- Many bounces → clean the list, remove bad addresses.
- Test emails land in spam.
- Someone says you are spamming them.

**How to send:**
- Send one by one from your normal inbox. No bulk-email tools for now.
- Wait 2–5 minutes between emails. Don't send 15 emails in one minute.
- Plain text, no images, no attachments, no open-tracking, 1 link maximum.
- Don't change the domain's email settings (DNS) while it is working.
- Weekdays only.

### 6.2 Daily routine (about 2.5 hours)

| Time (Texas) | How long | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 6:30 am | 15 min | **Replies first.** Answer every reply. Mark every "no" as `do_not_contact`. |
| 6:45 am | 30 min | **Send follow-ups due today.** Filter `prospects.csv` where `next_step` date is today or earlier. |
| 7:15 am | 30 min | **Send 10–15 new first emails** (weeks 1–2). Check each company quickly first: website status, city, one true personal line. |
| Afternoon | 30 min | **Research tomorrow's companies.** Add to `prospects.csv`. Skip franchises, big companies with good websites, and companies outside DFW. |
| 12:00–1:00 pm or 4:00–6:00 pm | 30–45 min | **Calls:** 5–10 companies. |
| Any time | — | **Drafts** for people who said yes (these come first, before everything else). |
| End of day | 10 min | **Update `prospects.csv`.** Every row you touched today. |

**Every Friday (15 minutes):** count emails sent, replies, "yes" answers, drafts sent, and deals won.
If you get fewer than 2 replies per 100 emails, **change the subject line or the first lines. Do not send more emails.**

### 6.3 Tracking in `prospects.csv`

Update the row **right after every contact**. Use these three columns:

**`status`** (use only these words):

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `new` | Found, not checked yet |
| `researched` | Checked: real DFW company, website status known |
| `email_1` / `email_2` / `email_3` | Last email sent |
| `called` | Called (with or without answer) |
| `replied` | They answered. Read and respond. |
| `interested` | They said yes or asked questions |
| `draft_in_progress` | You have their info, draft is being built |
| `draft_sent` | Draft sent, waiting for feedback |
| `won` | Signed |
| `lost` | Said no after talking, or chose someone else |

**`last_contact`**: the date of your last email, call or text. Format: `YYYY-MM-DD` (example: `2026-10-13`).

**`next_step`**: start with the date, then the action, so you can sort by it.
Examples: `2026-10-16 email_2`, `2026-10-22 email_3`, `2026-10-15 send draft by 5pm`, `2026-10-14 call 4pm`.

## 7. Honesty rules

These rules protect your name. One lie found by one fence owner can spread to many others. They talk to each other.

1. **Never claim past clients.** No "we've helped dozens of companies". Until you have real clients, say "We're new, and that's why the draft is free."
2. **Never fake reviews or testimonials.** Not on your website, not on Facebook, not on Google, not from friends or family. Never buy followers or likes.
3. **Never imply a Texas office.** Don't say "Dallas-based", "local", or "our Texas office". Say: "a remote team that works Texas business hours." A rented US mailbox is a "mailing address", never an "office".
4. **Don't make the team bigger than it is.** If it's just you, say "I".
5. **Demo sites are fictional.** Lone Oak, Prairie Post and Caliche Road are made-up companies. Always say "example design", never "our client".
6. **No fake urgency.** Mention "$0 setup for the first 3 partners" only while it's true. Remove it from emails and from the website when the spots are gone.
7. **Every personal line must be true.** Never say "a friend recommended you" or "I drove by your job" unless it really happened.
8. **Never say "re:" or "fwd:"** on a first email.
9. **Facts must be real.** Don't invent numbers. If you use "about 165,000 searches a month for 'fence company near me'", say "about", like the website does.
10. **In drafts, use only the owner's real information.** No made-up reviews, no "licensed and insured" unless they say so, no other company's photos.
11. **Price is always clear,** including the 12-month minimum, before anyone signs or pays.
12. **"No" means no.** Stop the same day, on every channel, forever.

## 8. Social media: should you create pages? (yes) + 3 posts

### 8.1 Short answer: yes, but keep it small

Fence owners will check you before they reply. Many of your prospects live on Facebook. A page with a logo, a cover photo and a few real posts makes you look like a real business. An empty page, or no page, looks risky.

| Platform | Do it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook Page** | **Yes, first** | This is where fence owners are. Many of your prospects use Facebook as their "website". |
| **LinkedIn** (your profile + a company page) | Yes | Some people will check *you*, the person. Shows you are real. |
| Instagram | Optional | Same posts as Facebook, if you have time. |
| Google Business Profile | **Not now** | Google expects a real local address and a service area near it. Don't use a virtual Texas address. That breaks Google's rules and our honesty rule 3. |
| Nextdoor | No | Made for local businesses with a local address. |
